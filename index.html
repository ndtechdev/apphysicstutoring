About Us

Meet your tutors

We are students who have taken AP Physics and understand what makes the class challenging.

Nishtha & Ojal

Hi! Our names are Nishtha and Ojal. We both completed AP Physics 1 at Irvington High School with Mr. Lee, a teacher known for having one of the most challenging AP Physics courses, and we earned A’s both semesters.

Through this experience, we became familiar with the types of questions, problem-solving methods, and concepts that students are expected to understand. We also have access to our own preparation materials, practice problems, review strategies, and College Board-style practice questions.

In addition to taking AP Physics ourselves, we have tutored elementary students in introductory physics, giving us experience explaining new and difficult concepts in a simple, clear, and supportive way.

We are both taking Physics C next year and want to help students feel more confident and prepared before entering AP Physics.

Our Vision

AP Physics can feel overwhelming at first because it combines conceptual science, algebra, trigonometry, and problem-solving. Our goal is to help students build a strong foundation before the school year begins, so they can walk into class with more confidence, stronger skills, and a better understanding of how to approach physics problems.

We want students to not only learn the formulas, but also understand when to use them, how to break down difficult questions, and how to prepare effectively for quizzes, tests, and AP-style problems.

Curriculum

8-Week AP Physics Prep Plan

This curriculum is designed to build a strong foundation before AP Physics begins. Students will review the math skills, core concepts, and problem-solving strategies needed for success in AP Physics.

Week 1

Foundations & Kinematics

Class 1: Foundations

  • Scalars vs. vectors
  • Magnitude and direction
  • Trig: sin, cos, tan, SOH-CAH-TOA
  • Component decomposition
  • Dimensional analysis and significant figures

Class 2: Kinematics

  • Kinematic equations
  • Free fall
  • Motion graphs: position-time, velocity-time, acceleration-time
Week 2

Kinematics & Newton’s Laws

Class 3: Kinematics Part 2

  • 2D projectile motion
  • Practice multiple-choice questions
  • Practice FRQ-style question

Class 4: Forces & Newton’s Laws

  • Newton’s 1st and 2nd laws
  • Free-body diagrams
  • Normal force, static friction, kinetic friction, and tension
  • Vector addition in free-body diagrams
  • Newton’s 3rd law and systems of objects
  • Inclined planes and Atwood machine examples
Week 3

Circular Motion, Gravity & Force Practice

Class 5: Circular Motion & Gravitation

  • Uniform circular motion
  • Centripetal acceleration and centripetal force
  • Newton’s law of gravitation
  • Kepler’s laws

Class 6: Forces Practice

  • Force problem-solving practice
  • Free-body diagram review
  • Mixed Newton’s laws problems
  • Conceptual and calculation-based practice
Week 4

Energy

1 Month Mark

Class 7: Work & Energy

  • Work-energy theorem
  • Kinetic energy and potential energy
  • Conservation of energy
  • Power

Class 8: Springs & Energy Systems

  • Energy in springs
  • Hooke’s law
  • Energy bar charts
  • Non-conservative forces
  • Energy loss
Week 5

Momentum

Class 9: Impulse & 1D Momentum

  • Impulse-momentum theorem
  • Conservation of momentum
  • 1D collisions: elastic vs. inelastic
  • Identifying which conservation law applies

Class 10: 2D Momentum

  • 2D collisions
  • Momentum conserved independently in x and y directions
  • Vector components in momentum problems
  • Center of mass
  • Explosions
Week 6

Torque & Rotational Motion

Class 11: Torque & Rotational Dynamics

  • Torque: τ = rF sin θ
  • Trig in torque problems
  • Rotational inertia
  • Newton’s 2nd law for rotation: τ = Iα
  • Lever arm
  • Rotational free-body diagrams

Class 12: Equilibrium & Rolling

  • Static equilibrium
  • Translational equilibrium conditions
  • Rotational equilibrium conditions
  • Rolling without slipping
  • Combined linear and rotational systems
Week 7

Rotational Energy & Angular Momentum

Class 13: Rotational Energy & Angular Momentum

  • Rotational kinetic energy: ½Iω²
  • Angular momentum: L = Iω
  • Conservation of angular momentum
  • Spinning skater and planet examples

Class 14: Combined Rotation Problems

  • Combined translational and rotational energy
  • Rolling objects
  • Angular impulse
  • Connecting linear and rotational momentum conservation
Week 8

SHM & Fluids

Class 15: Simple Harmonic Motion

  • Simple harmonic motion
  • Springs and pendulums
  • Period and frequency
  • Restoring force
  • Energy in SHM
  • Graphs of position, velocity, and acceleration vs. time

Class 16: Fluids

  • Density and pressure
  • Pascal’s law
  • Buoyancy and Archimedes’ principle
  • Bernoulli’s equation
  • Continuity equation
